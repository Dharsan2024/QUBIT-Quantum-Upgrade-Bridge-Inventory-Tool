A single kappa would misrepresent the label set in both directions, and a second independent human rater (50 blind items) now replaces the intra-rater figure this pack previously had to substitute. Whether an algorithm is present at all agrees almost perfectly both against the model (κ = 0.960) and against a second human (κ = 0.957) -- which is what every false-positive claim in this study rests on. The use/mention boundary is a genuinely harder call: the primary annotator agrees with the model at κ = 0.575 but with an independent second human at only κ = 0.399, and the disagreement runs the SAME direction in both comparisons -- the primary annotator calls USE where the model and the second rater both call MENTION more often than the reverse. That revises the earlier reading ("the model is conservative"): the more likely account is that the primary annotator's own USE threshold runs looser than an independent rater's, not that the model under-calls real uses.
